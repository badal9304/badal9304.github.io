## Context and scope

Rupyy, a fintech platform under CarDekho, helps people get vehicle loans online. It serves two groups: customers exploring loans on the website, and dealers helping walk-in buyers at showrooms. The homepage and loan journey were unclear, not very engaging and not transparent about eligibility, which led to high drop-offs and wasted effort.

This case study covers two pieces of work:

- **Homepage redesign:** a stronger first impression, a clear value proposition, and prompts to act: an eligibility check, an EMI calculator and a sticky call to action.
- **Two-wheeler loan journey:** separate customer and dealer flows with an early eligibility check, less manual work for dealers, and lower running costs.

**Scope:** UI/UX improvements, competitor benchmarking, and flow redesigns to build trust, reduce friction and raise conversions.

## The homepage problem

People landing on the Rupyy homepage couldn’t find a clear, action-led way into the loan journey. The page had nothing that prompted them to act straight away, little to build trust, and weak mobile usability. That made it harder work to use and led to drop-offs.

### User needs and pain points

1. **Weak first impression.** The hero section looked dated and lacked emotional pull. The first scroll didn’t create excitement or show credibility, which matters for a financial product.
2. **Unclear value proposition.** No bold headline said what Rupyy offers or why it matters. On mobile, where the banner is small, it didn’t read as a loan platform.
3. **No clear starting point.** There was no prompt such as “Check loan eligibility”.
4. **English only.** The platform didn’t serve Tier 2 and Tier 3 city users, who may prefer Hindi or a regional language.
5. **Dated UI.** No 3D icons or animated illustrations. It felt template-like next to KreditBee, Slice and Navi.
6. **No loan calculator.** People like to play with the numbers, and an EMI calculator helps engagement and understanding.
7. **No sticky call to action.** No floating “Check eligibility” button followed the user down the page.
8. **Testimonials lacked emotion.** Generic cards with no faces, cars or real-life photos did little to build trust with aspirational buyers.

### Competitor analysis

I looked at BankBazaar, Navi, KreditBee and Paisabazaar. All three patterns below were common to them:

1. An instant eligibility check up front
2. An EMI calculator on the homepage
3. A clean, mobile-first UI with a clear visual hierarchy

## The loan journey problem

In the original two-wheeler loan process, users didn’t know whether they were eligible, or for how much, even after submitting all their details. That caused confusion and drop-offs and slowed the journey down. Dealers had extra manual work and delays because they couldn’t see a customer’s eligibility early on.

![The original two-wheeler loan flow](/images/rupyy/loan-current-flow.jpg)

### Solution

1. **CIBIL check.** Bringing the credit check forward tells users straight away whether they’re eligible and for how much. It makes the process more transparent, reduces drop-offs, and saves dealers time on manual checks.
2. **Two separate flows:** one for dealers, and one for customers who come through the website and complete the loan journey on their own.

## Dealer journey

![Dealer loan flow screens](/images/rupyy/dealer-flow.png)

**Flow**

1. OTP verification
2. Basic customer details (name, date of birth, pin code, PAN)
3. CIBIL score, eligible loan amount, and choice of loan amount
4. Choose lender
5. Choose vehicle
6. Additional customer details (gender, email, monthly income, address)
7. Review and submit

**Pros**

- **Saves dealer time.** Dealers know straight away whether to push the finance option or drop it, and don’t spend time on customers with low eligibility.
- **Faster qualification.** Sales staff can qualify leads faster and suggest a better alternative, such as a cash deal.
- **Less work.** Dealers don’t need to finish full KYC before they know the outcome.

**Cons**

- **CIBIL drop-offs.** Customers with low credit scores may feel discouraged and leave. Dealers can handle this reassuringly, for example: “Let me check with our finance manager to explore other options.”

## Customer journey

![Customer loan flow screens](/images/rupyy/customer-flow.png)

**Flow**

1. OTP verification
2. Choose vehicle
3. Customer details (name, date of birth, pin code, PAN and more)
4. Choose loan amount
5. CIBIL score, eligible loan amount, and choice of lender
6. Review and submit
7. Confirmation screen

**Pros**

- **Better experience.** Users stay engaged and can explore before seeing any rejection. By the time the CIBIL score comes back, they have already put in effort, which reduces drop-off.
- **More data collected.** Even if the credit score is low, there are enough details to retarget or call the user.
- **Easier retargeting.** The business captures email, PAN, mobile number and vehicle preference, even from unqualified leads.

**Cons**

- **Backend cost.** The CIBIL and lender-matching APIs run after more data is collected, so infrastructure costs are slightly higher.
- **Disappointment if no lender matches.** If a low credit score means no lender matches, the user sees: “We’re reviewing your application and will get in touch with the best possible offer. Stay tuned!”

## Why two separate flows

1. **CIBIL checks cost money.** Running one for every user adds backend and running costs.
2. **Dealer walk-ins are high intent.** People who visit a showroom usually mean to buy, so it’s worth showing their CIBIL score and lender options early.
3. **Website visitors are exploring.** Most are still browsing. Running a CIBIL check too early for them would add a lot of cost for little return.
4. **The flow is designed around cost.** To avoid wasted credit checks, the customer flow runs the CIBIL check at the end, once basic details are in and intent is clearer.
